- **Load-in hours:** Thursday 8 AM–8 PM · Friday 8 AM–11 AM

## What to Bring

Lights · extension cords/power strips · barrier products (machine bags, clip cord sleeves, bottle bags, sensi-wrap, drape cloths, dental bibs) · sterile dressing · surface disinfectant (Madacide/Envirocide) · green soap · gloves · armrests · aftercare instructions · banners · portfolios/flash books

## Veteran Artists

**Save $150 on your booth** if a veteran is tattooing or vending in it. Include proof of veteran status with your application to claim the discount. It's a small thank-you to those who've served — and it applies to any booth type.

## Health Department & Permitting

Every artist tattooing at AATC must be permitted by the county health department — even if you already hold a valid Fayetteville tattoo permit, the convention counts as a new location and requires a guest-artist permit.

- Upload a photo ID with your application. **No submitted ID = no tattooing at the convention.** We submit all permitting paperwork for you — your only job on-site is booth inspection after you arrive.
- All artists must be set up and ready for inspection by **Friday at 9:00 AM**. Early permitting is available **Thursday, 1:00–5:00 PM**.
- **Disposable tubes/cartridges only.** No metal tubes.
- Sterile dressing must be used during the event.
- No microblading, permanent makeup, or piercing without prior authorization.

## Payment Terms

Upon receipt of your invoice, a **25% deposit** is due, with the remainder paid by **January 1**. Booths are not held if payment deadlines are missed. Deposits are non-refundable, and payments do not roll over if you cancel.

# Home of the Free, Because of the Brave

The goal of the All American Tattoo Convention is to give some of the bravest heroes in the world the opportunity to be tattooed by some of the best tattoo artists in the industry.

Tattoos and the US armed forces share a history dating back to the early 1900s. For the millions of Americans who have served — active duty and reservists alike — ink is a living record: loyalty to a branch and a unit, pride in country, camaraderie with fellow soldiers, and remembrance of the fallen.

Tattoos and body art tell stories that words fail to serve. No one knows this better than our troops, who often come home from war struggling to rejoin everyday life and relate to those who have no concept of what it's like. Tattoos give these veterans an outlet to express themselves and to show the pride of being part of something bigger than themselves.
